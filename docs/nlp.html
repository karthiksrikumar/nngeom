NLP geometry

Ten tools that treat a language model's hidden states as geometric objects: tokens travel through layers, prompts trace paths, senses form clusters, and degeneration orbits attractors. All of them take plain numpy arrays, so they work with hidden states extracted from any transformer.

Getting hidden states. With HuggingFace: out = model(**inputs, output_hidden_states=True) gives a tuple of layers; stack one sequence into an array of shape (n_layers, n_tokens, dim) and everything below applies unchanged. The same shapes come out of torch hooks or JAX models.

1Token trajectory

ng.token_trajectory(hidden_states, token_index)

Follows one token through every layer of the residual stream and measures the path it traces: total length, straightness (tortuosity), turning angles, and the layer where the biggest transformation happens. Tokens the model works hard to resolve (rare words, ambiguous references) travel farther and turn more; function words barely move. Comparing trajectories across tokens gives a per-token map of where the model spends its representational effort.

traj = ng.token_trajectory(hidden, token_index=5)
traj["path_length"], traj["busiest_layer"], traj["tortuosity"]

2Contextualization score

ng.contextualization_score(static_embedding, contexts)

Quantifies how much context reshapes a token. Collect the same token's vector from many different sentences and compare against its static (layer-0) embedding: the mean shift says how contextual the token is, and the self-similarity across contexts says how consistently the model treats it. Low self-similarity flags tokens whose meaning the model resolves very differently per sentence.

3Prompt divergence

ng.prompt_divergence(hidden_a, hidden_b)

Finds the depth at which the model starts treating two prompts differently. Feed the hidden states of two related prompts (a question phrased two ways, with and without a system instruction, benign vs adversarial phrasing) and get the per-layer distance between their final-token states plus the first layer of meaningful divergence. Shallow divergence means surface wording changed the computation early; late divergence means the model processed them identically until the end. A practical lens on prompt sensitivity and jailbreak analysis.

div = ng.prompt_divergence(hidden_a, hidden_b)
div["divergence_layer"], div["per_layer_relative"]

4Sentence curvature

ng.sentence_curvature(embeddings)

Treats a sentence as a curve: token embeddings visited in order within one layer. Reports mean and max turning angle, tortuosity, and step sizes. Coherent prose forms smoother paths; syntactically chaotic, code-switched, or adversarial inputs turn sharply. Useful as a cheap, model-internal fluency and anomaly signal.

5Polysemy score

ng.polysemy_score(contexts, max_senses)

Estimates how many distinct senses a token exhibits, purely from geometry. Clusters the token's contextual embeddings across many sentences and picks the sense count by the elbow of within-cluster dispersion. Word sense induction with no lexicon, no labels, and no extra model: "bank" shows two tight clusters, "the" shows one.

senses = ng.polysemy_score(contextual_vectors_of_bank)
senses["n_senses"], senses["assignments"]

6Layer transition profile

ng.layer_transition_profile(hidden_states)

Computes CKA of every layer against the embedding layer (input-likeness) and against the final layer (output-likeness). The crossing point is the network's processing waist: the depth where it stops encoding the input and starts building the output. Comparing transition layers across models, sizes, or fine-tunes shows where the division of labor moved.

7Isotropy correction

ng.isotropy_correction(embeddings, n_components)

Transformer embeddings are notoriously anisotropic: everything points into a narrow cone, which wrecks cosine similarity. This implements all-but-the-top (mean removal plus top principal component removal) and reports anisotropy before and after, so you can measure exactly how much usable geometry the correction unlocks before feeding embeddings into retrieval or clustering.

8Semantic axes and projection

ng.semantic_axis(positive, negative) and ng.semantic_projection(embeddings, axis)

Build a direction from a contrast set (formal vs casual sentences, positive vs negative reviews, toxic vs benign) and score any embedding along it. The scores are signed projections, so both ordering and magnitude are meaningful. This is the geometric skeleton of steering vectors and bias probes, in two lines.

axis = ng.semantic_axis(formal_embs, casual_embs)
formality_scores = ng.semantic_projection(sentence_embs, axis)

9Repetition attractor score

ng.repetition_attractor_score(embeddings)

Detects degeneration by its geometric signature: a generation stuck in a loop revisits the same region of representation space. Measures the recurrence rate of well-separated token pairs whose states are unusually close. Healthy text keeps exploring; looping text orbits an attractor. Works on the hidden states themselves, so it can flag degeneration before it is obvious in the surface text.

10Token novelty

ng.token_novelty(embeddings, window)

Per-token geometric surprise: the residual of projecting each token's embedding onto the subspace spanned by everything said so far. High-novelty tokens introduce genuinely new directions: topic shifts, new entities, or hallucination onsets. A sliding window restricts the memory to recent context.

novelty = ng.token_novelty(last_layer_states)
suspicious = novelty > novelty.mean() + 2 * novelty.std()

+One-call profile

ng.nlp_summary(hidden_states) runs the transition profile, anisotropy, sentence curvature, repetition, and novelty in one call and returns a single dict, handy for logging a geometric health check per generation.
